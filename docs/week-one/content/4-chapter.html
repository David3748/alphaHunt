Chapter 4 · The real test
Seventeen years, and the original strategy fails

2009–2025: 350,456 structured filings filtered to 9,885 eligible events, each read by five specialist lenses (liquidity, operations, catalysts, accounting, governance) and synthesized twice, roughly 69,000 calls running unattended on a VM while I slept. The originally deployed score ran first against gates frozen weeks earlier.

It failed. Sharpe 0.74 versus a required 1.0. Max drawdown −31.8% versus −25%. Publishing that feels bad. It's also the point: the gates existed precisely so that moment would be a non-event instead of a temptation.

Three replacement rules had been pre-registered before any outcome opened, tested only on holdout cohorts with discovery years excluded and Bonferroni correction across exactly three hypotheses:

The row that matters is the mechanical baseline losing. "Buy whatever is crushed" earned −0.09 IR over seventeen years while the model's probability fields earned about +1.5. (IR is information ratio: excess return per unit of risk, exposures matched. Half a point is respectable; 1.5 made me re-check my own code.) Whatever the signal is, it isn't repackaged dip-buying.
