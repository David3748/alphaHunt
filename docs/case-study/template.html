Item 1

The question

When a stock has already fallen 40% or more, the market has priced in a story. Its filings, including the liquidity runway, covenant headroom, backlog and footnotes, say whether that story is right. Reading them carefully is slow, and that is where an LLM should help.

No document from the future

A bitemporal store records when each fact became public and when it was recorded. Every forecast is pinned to a hashed manifest that rejects anything accepted after its knowledge time.

No survivorship

Eligibility never needs a future price. A delisted stock is marked to zero at its first missing close.

No forking paths

Rules, cohorts and pass/fail gates were written down and frozen before outcomes were opened. 2019–20 was used for discovery and excluded from confirmation.

No luck by clustering

500 placebos shuffle scores within calendar months, preserving regime timing, and are judged on 2009–18 and 2021–25 holdouts.

No expensive momentum screen

Drawdown, momentum, volatility and size are first-class baselines in walk-forward models.

No hallucinated evidence

Claims whose quote isn’t in the filing are dropped from forecast inputs. About two-thirds survive.

The blind test rules out the easy channel. It can’t rule out recognition from products, drugs, subsidiaries, mines or former names, and the identification probe shows that channel is wide open even for a small model reading a fraction of the evidence. Its misses often had the right clues and the wrong name (Altisource’s Hubzu and Ocwen, CEL-SCI’s Multikine), so this rate is a floor.

Given just a name and a date, it remembers what came next

One hundred fresh Haiku contexts each got only a company, a ticker and a filing date, then answered how the stock did over the next 90 days vs SPY. No filing text, no prices. Eighty cases were big 2011–24 moves, half up and half down. Twenty were 2026 moves the model can’t have seen.

A filing’s date and industry are enough to recall what the market did next, and no redaction removes them. So an LLM backtest on data from before the model’s training cutoff can’t be taken at face value. How much it overstates skill is a separate question, and answering it needs a control.

Memory or market?

The last test holds the model, the prompt and the evidence fixed and changes only whether the outcome is in Haiku’s training data. One hundred fresh Haiku contexts each forecast one filing from 10,000 scrubbed characters of MD&A, under the backtest forecaster’s own instruction to use nothing but the excerpt. Fifty filings are from 2023 to September 2024, so their 90-day outcomes are in Haiku’s training data. The other fifty are from August 2025 on. Each half has 25 stocks that beat SPY by 25+ points and 25 that trailed by 25+.

Item 7

Are the probabilities probabilities?

Not quite. The model is timid: every decile of its forecasts lands above the diagonal, so events it calls 10% happen about 15% of the time.

PeriodMean forecastObservedBrier skillAfter recalibration

Brier skill is measured against the base rate of strictly earlier years. Recalibration is a Platt scaling refit each year on past years only. It roughly doubles historical skill, from tiny to small. In 2026 no rescaling helps, because the forecasts stopped separating outcomes.

Item 8

What I’d do differently

  1. Only score questions after the model’s cutoff. Use models with published training cutoffs, keep a rolling live cohort, and treat everything earlier as development data, however clean the pipeline. Redaction can’t close the date channel.

  2. Make re-identification a gate, not a footnote. Before a case enters a backtest, an adversarial “name this company” model has to fail on the evidence. Neutralize subsidiaries, places and products, not just the registrant name.

  3. Separate leakage from regime, at scale. Seven of the 12 live losers came in February and March 2026, several of them software names. Exhibit G’s design is the right one, but with 50 filings per period its interval spans ±0.3 AUC. Run it with the production forecaster on about a thousand filings, with sector-neutral scoring.

  4. Trade the part of the signal that generalizes. The information sits in the middle of the ranking. A quintile-spread or long/short book uses it. A top-decile long book depends on the tail.

  5. Use the model to audit the data. The identification probe exposed a symbol-resolution bug: 23 non-traded REITs had inherited the ticker ARCT and were scored on another company’s prices. A full audit then found of cases priced on the wrong stock. These included small companies priced as Ford, because their filing agent names files “f10q…” and the resolver fell back to the file name. The fix is a CIK-keyed security master.

  6. Treat calibration as its own model. Raw LLM probabilities are rank scores. A walk-forward recalibration layer, and ensembles across model families, should sit between the agents and any decision.
