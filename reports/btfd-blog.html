TL;DR

We taught an LLM to read SEC filings like a forensic accountant, aimed it at stocks that had crashed 40%+ from their highs, and then spent most of our time trying to prove ourselves wrong. Two strategies died in sealed validation. One survived. The deployed ranking rule turned out to be the weakest of four rules we already had on disk, a one-line mechanical "deepest drawdown" filter beat the model on raw return, and all of the early alpha came from a single V-shaped crash. So we froze our hypotheses before looking at outcomes and scaled the whole thing to 350,456 filings across 2009–2025. The frozen P(+20%) rule now shows ~×710 compounded vs SPY's ×10.2 in the sealed corpus evaluation — and we still won't call it a strategy until the bear-market cohort and the placebo gauntlet say so.

Prologue Why "forensic"?

The F-word in the title isn't the one Reddit uses. The one that summarizes this process is forensic. Every claim the model makes must be grounded to an exact quote in a real filing. Every prediction is frozen to a content-addressed manifest of exactly what was knowable at the time. Every hypothesis is sealed before outcomes are opened. The dossiers it writes read like forensic accounting reports — because they are.

A taste, from the Arbutus Biopharma (ABUS) dossier, verdict TROUBLE: a $230M Dutch-auction tender with no disclosed funding source, sitting on top of non-cash royalty revenue contractually capped on both ends, a winding-down R&D org, and two litigation-settlement-shaped subsequent events disclosed five weeks before the filing. Eight red flags, each one traced to a specific 10-Q or 8-K. That's the house style: no vibes, only citations.

Act I The hunt: 500 dossiers and a merger-arb microscope

It started as a shotgun. A full-text sweep of SEC EDGAR produced a dossiers/ directory with over 500 single-ticker forensic write-ups, a deep report that underwrote live merger events deal-by-deal, and an event-driven trade board. The model killed most of what it touched — correctly:

  • TBPH / Zymeworks: $17.00 cash + a CVR the market priced at ≈$0. The model agreed with the market — the CVR's underlying drug had already failed Phase 3. Thesis killed.
  • TECH / Merck KGaA: $73.00 cash, but the spread was ~0.94% gross for up to 18 months of carry plus an undisclosed regulatory path. Efficient. Stand down.
  • MCFN reverse merger: a 1:1 stock swap into a holdco where the CEO sits on both sides and keeps 80% of the vote. No spread, no trade, next.

That phase produced the tooling, the skeptic/analyst prompt pattern, and a healthy disrespect for headline numbers. What it didn't produce was a repeatable, testable signal. For that you need data infrastructure that can't lie to you.

Act II The lab: a database that remembers what it didn't know

The core problem with backtesting LLM research: the future leaks in everywhere. Revised filings, survivorship-biased ticker lists, "as of" dates that silently use restated data. So we built a bitemporal temporal store where every knowledge-bearing record carries three clocks:

  • effective_at — when the fact is economically true;
  • available_at — the earliest moment a market participant could know it (enriched to exact SEC acceptance timestamps, not just filing dates — a fix that moved some knowledge cutoffs by 13+ hours);
  • system_from — when this database recorded that version, append-only, history derived with LEAD().

Every prediction must cite a frozen input_manifest of exact document versions and content hashes; the store rejects any manifest containing a document that wasn't yet available. Large payloads live as immutable SHA-256 blobs; the whole thing exports to Parquet; a validator re-hashes every blob and audits for lookahead, orphans, and version violations. By the latest ledger: 19,001 grounded claim versions, 760 experiment cases, 3,540 analysis runs, 1,320 predictions, DuckDB at 96 MB, a 2.1 GB immutable corpus — with 56/56 unit tests and zero integrity issues.

Rule zero, enforced in code: an API key is never written to a file, a log, a command, or the database. Protocols are frozen in writing before outcomes are opened. Always.

Act III The graveyard: two strategies die on schedule

With infrastructure you can trust, failure becomes informative. We failed twice, cleanly:

lab_runs/dilution90
Dilution-event forecast. 120 cases, AUC 0.907 — gorgeous — but high-confidence event precision was only 55.6%. Not production proof.
lab_runs/long_dev
False-distress long ("buy crashed stocks the LLM likes"). Dev AUC 0.646; sealed validation AUC 0.614, top decile 0/4 successes, −3.7% excess. Verdict: failed validation, retained as a reproducible negative experiment.
lab_runs/comprehensive_long
The big one: 2,860 fresh model calls (220 cases × 5 specialist lenses × 2 replicates, plus 660 syntheses) — 20,429 proposed claims, 14,037 exact-quote-grounded (68.7%), 1,490 raw filings fetched with zero failures. Sealed validation still failed: AUC 0.566, top basket zero successes. The four highest-ranked names — RKLB, VSTM, CHWY, UI — returned +16.9%, −26.3%, +11.4%, −12.4%. Nobody met the bar.

The comprehensive run did leave one smoking clue behind: inside the dilution-conditioned cohort, the model's top decile returned +28.4% mean excess with a 41.7% hit rate — but with a permutation p≈0.106, that was exploratory, not evidence. File under: hypotheses.

Act IV The signal: safety-first, a.k.a. BTFD with a seatbelt

The formulation that survived inverted the question. Don't ask the model which crashed stock will moon. Ask it which crashed stock won't die: severe drawdown (−40% off the 1-year high), liquid, >$1 — then rank by the model's downside-tail probability, lowest first. Ten slots, 10% of NAV each, next-session entry, 90-day holds, 10–25 bp costs, idle cash earning the 13-week T-bill rate. A full robustness battery: causal expanding thresholds, hold-period sweeps, cost sweeps, delays, leave-one-out, block bootstraps, 2,000 random baskets.

So we ran the honest next step: a fresh confirmation on untouched 2021–2023 data with locked thresholds. 1,140 provider requests, 14.2M prompt tokens, 4,964 grounded claims later:

Fresh confirmation verdict

Safety-first confirmed. Top-8 mean excess +29.1%, median +31.8%, 75% hit the +20% success bar, +33.8-point spread vs the rest, one-sided permutation p=0.0029 — passing even the Bonferroni p<0.025 gate. Robustness: AUC 0.743, Spearman 0.484, positive means for basket sizes 4–20.

Dilution-event-conditioned did not confirm (p=0.1714). One for two. The lab notebook keeps both.

In a portfolio simulation, swapping the ranker roughly doubled excess return, and the best simple blend — upside × depth of drawdown — reached +35.8% mean per-trade excess. The LLM's unique edge is hit rate — it does exactly what it was asked to do (avoid losers) — while the raw money in a V-shaped recovery came from buying the deepest dips, period. Worse, essentially all of the 2019–2020 alpha was 2020's alpha (2019 matched IR: 0.0125, i.e. zero), and the eligibility rule is an accidental crash detector: 98 of 188 signals fired in March–May 2020. One crash, and it was the best-case one.

Lesson carved on the lab wall: mechanical baselines get promoted to first-class gates. The model must beat "deepest drawdown" and "one line of SQL," not just a random-number generator.

Act VI The fix: freeze the hypotheses, then scale to a century

Before opening any century-scale outcomes, we froze CENTURY_HYPOTHESES.md: the secondary rankers (p_plus20, an equal-weight causal z-blend, and upside × drawdown), the mechanical deep-drawdown comparator, Bonferroni α=0.0167, 500 blocked placebos per rule, and — critically — 2019–2020 quarantined as the discovery cohort, with 2009–2018 and 2021–2025 as the two confirmatory holdouts. The bear-market question (2009–2012) is the whole ballgame: if the signal survives a grinding bear it's a strategy; if not, it's a dip-buying rule fit to the best dip in history.

Then we built the machine to answer it. An Azure VM (Standard_B4as_v2, SSH locked to a single IP, API key held only in process memory) running a resumable tmux-orchestrated pipeline:

The fine print we wrote ourselves

Short-term tax drag is unmodeled and likely larger than the 25 bp cost assumption. The score is lumpy (64 distinct values across 1,713 cases; 99 cases tied at exactly 18%). The 90-day hold sits on the peak of the horizon curve. And the confirmation cohorts must still speak — especially 2009–2012. None of the headline multiples matter until the bear signs off.

Epilogue What "buy the forensic dip" actually means

The process, distilled:

  • Buy the dip, but only the dips a forensic reader of the filings can't kill — funding runway verified, catalyst dated, accounting and governance vetoes passed.
  • Freeze the question before peeking at the answer. Sealed protocols, discovery cohorts quarantined, Bonferroni-corrected gates, 500 placebos shuffled inside calendar months.
  • Make failure cheap and permanent. Two dead strategies are stored, validated, and exportable — negative results with full lineage, not deleted embarrassment.
  • Make the model earn its calls. Same-prompt replicates correlate 0.78–0.95, so budget goes to more issuers, claim-change extraction, and adversarial verification of the top decile — not to asking the same question twice.
  • Never trust a number without a baseline. If one line of SQL ("deepest drawdown") beats your model on return, say so in the report — we did, in print, above.

The dip is a dangerous place. That's the point. Bring a forensic accountant.
