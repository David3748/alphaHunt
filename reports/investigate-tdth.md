## Verdict: TROUBLE
**Trident Digital is executing a classic cap-table reset — a 240:1 reverse split, a 24x authorized-share expansion, and an undisclosed-subscriber private placement — while quietly dismantling its US ADS structure**

- A 240-to-1 share consolidation was approved — an extreme ratio far beyond typical Nasdaq-compliance splits (1:10–1:50), implying severe price deterioration and/or massive prior dilution
- Authorized share capital expanded 24-fold (US$50,000 → US$1,200,000; 5B → 120B shares) for vague 'future issuances' headroom, leaving near-unlimited dilution capacity at board discretion even on a consolidated-equivalent basis (~500M vs ~20.8M prior)
- Fractional-share cancellation zeroes out any holder of fewer than 120 pre-consolidation shares with no disclosed cash-in-lieu — an embedded minority squeeze-out mechanism
- The sponsored ADS program (with Citibank, established Sept 2024) is being terminated after ~22 months with no stated rationale — frequently a precursor to delisting, going-private, or restructuring
- A Share Subscription Agreement was approved in the same EGM as the split, charter restatement, and authorized-share increase, but the subscriber's identity, price, and discount are not visible in these filings — sequencing strongly suggests new possibly related-party capital entering immediately after the reset
- 56M post-consolidation Class A/C shares were redesignated as 'blank shares,' giving the board unilateral discretion to create new classes or shift control without a fresh shareholder vote
- This is the third full restatement of the Memorandum & Articles, and all filings are signed solely by one person holding both Chairman and CEO titles — concentrated authority with weak internal checks

Red flags: 8

The aggregate architecture — extreme reverse split + 24x authorized expansion + opaque private placement + ADS termination — is the signature of a distressed recapitalization that resets the cap table ahead of further dilutive or control-shifting issuance, with elevated delisting/going-private risk. No long exposure should be considered until the subscription terms and listing status are fully disclosed; existing holders face structural squeeze-out risk via fractional cancellation.
