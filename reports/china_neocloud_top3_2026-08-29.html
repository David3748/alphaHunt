What the comp gap says

KC trades at roughly one-fifth of CoreWeave’s TTM EV/sales and below one-quarter of its EV/EBITDA. VNET and GDS also trade well below Equinix and Digital Realty. A partial convergence is plausible; full convergence is not. US peers have superior access to leading Nvidia systems, deeper capital markets, higher software monetization, longer disclosed backlogs and lower China/VIE/geopolitical discounts.

Do not use P/E mechanically

CoreWeave, KC and VNET are loss-making. Nebius’s small reported profit makes P/E absurd. EQIX/DLR GAAP earnings are depressed by depreciation. GDS earnings are affected by asset transactions. P/E is shown because it was requested; it is not the decision variable.

#1 compute

Kingsoft Cloud

The closest listed Chinese neocloud, although it also operates a conventional public and enterprise cloud.

Central question: does AI revenue become a recurring software-and-compute platform, or remain a capital-hungry server-rental business?

Why the market may be wrong

The share price is down roughly 25% over twelve months even as AI billings accelerated, public-cloud growth reached 45%, and operating profitability crossed zero. The market appears to value the revenue as low-quality hardware pass-through. If MaaS and orchestration raise gross profit per unit of installed compute, the appropriate multiple can rise before GAAP earnings arrive.

Why the market may be right

KC spent more on Q2 capex than it generated in Q2 revenue. Shares outstanding increased approximately 18% over the past year. A 15% gross margin offers little protection against falling compute prices, accelerator obsolescence or financing costs. The company can report positive EBITDA while destroying equity value if depreciation, leases and dilution are economically understated.

Quarterly proof points

  • AI billings growth versus public-cloud revenue growth: billing must remain ahead.
  • Gross profit growth versus depreciation: profit must outrun the installed fleet’s aging.
  • Capex/revenue below 70%, then below 50%, without supply constraints.
  • MaaS share, non-Xiaomi customer growth and customer concentration disclosure.
  • Cash plus committed financing relative to twelve-month capex plan.

Hidden operating leverage

At 73.9% utilization but 96.3% commitment, VNET has roughly 225 MW of committed, installed capacity not yet reflected as utilized. That spread is the most important leading indicator in the report. The value case works if move-ins occur faster than new debt service accumulates.

Balance-sheet trap

Net debt is approximately $3.57bn against a $1.86bn market capitalization. TTM capex was about 68% of revenue and FY26 guidance is higher still. A seemingly modest change in the EBITDA multiple creates enormous equity volatility because debt sits ahead of the equity.

Quarterly proof points

  • Incremental utilized MW and the time from contract signing to billing.
  • Capex funded by customer prepayments, project debt, REIT recycling or parent equity.
  • Revenue and EBITDA per utilized wholesale MW.
  • Interest expense growth versus adjusted EBITDA growth.
  • Customer concentration and cancellation protections on the 862 MW order book.

Asset-value support

GDS has previously monetized Chinese data-center assets at approximately 13–17× EBITDA through ABS and C-REIT structures. Those transaction multiples support the middle of the scenario range and provide a route to recycle capital without selling the entire platform.

Accounting warning

Trailing P/E and ROE overstate normalized earnings quality because reported profit includes gains connected with DayOne and other ownership changes. The cleaner recurring measures are adjusted EBITDA, monthly service revenue, utilization and cash interest coverage.

Quarterly proof points

  • Utilization crossing 80% while the commitment rate remains above 90%.
  • MRR stabilization after legacy-contract repricing.
  • Utility-cost growth below revenue growth.
  • Asset recycling at or above 13× EBITDA and use of proceeds for deleveraging.
  • China capex returns versus capital allocated to the DayOne stake.

AlphaHunt implementation

Pure compute expression: long KC. The trade depends on continued AI billing growth, improving gross margin and declining capital intensity per unit of revenue.

Lower-model-risk expression: long VNET and GDS. The underwriting relies on contracted capacity, delivery schedules, utilization and financing—not assumptions about which Chinese accelerator wins.

The Huawei caveat

None of the top three is a clean Huawei security. Huawei Cloud AICS is the actual Ascend neocloud, but Huawei is private.

For explicit Ascend exposure, use a smaller satellite basket of UCloud 688158 and Chinasoft 0354. UCloud offers Ascend 910B configurations; Chinasoft implements Huawei Cloud, CloudMatrix and Ascend workloads.
