Hungary vs. Brazil

Two high-real-rate easing cycles, separated by very different inflation dynamics. This dashboard maps recent moves, macro fundamentals, central-bank reaction functions and what the front-end curves actually price.

HUF
Hungary is the cleaner fundamental receiver; Brazil is the deeper carry market.

Hungary's policy–inflation gap is extreme, but fiscal credibility and EUR/HUF control the speed of cuts. Brazil offers much larger nominal carry, yet tight labor markets, unanchored expectations and debt dynamics justify a shallower cycle.

The MNB has cut from 6.50% at the start of the year to 5.50%, while inflation has fallen below the lower edge of its 3% ±1pp target band. Growth is positive but employment is softer year over year.

The obstacle is an inherited fiscal deficit potentially near 7.5% of GDP without additional measures. The MNB therefore treats forint stability and the risk premium as practical constraints on an otherwise obvious easing case.

Market messageThe nearly flat 5.3–5.4% curve prices only modest additional cuts and a persistent risk premium.

Copom has brought Selic down to 14.00%, but describes the cycle as cautious calibration. Current inflation has eased while expectations remain around 5.0% for 2026 and 4.2% for 2027, both above the 3% target.

A historically tight labor market supports services and consumption. Gross debt near 82% of GDP and enormous interest expense keep the long end above the policy rate despite the front-end easing.

Market messageThe curve prices roughly one more cut, then rates bottom near 13.6% before fiscal premium pushes them higher.

Central-bank reaction functions

MNB: Governor Mihály Varga; 3% ±1pp target. Formally inflation-driven, but practically constrained by EUR/HUF, fiscal credibility, EU funding, euro-convergence expectations and global risk appetite.

BCB/Copom: Governor Gabriel Galípolo; 3% ±1.5pp continuous target. Focuses on expectations, services inflation, the output gap, labor tightness, fiscal policy, BRL and oil. The August 25bp cut was unanimous among seven sitting members.

Preferred expressions

Hungary: receive 2Y HUF near 5.4%, or use a DV01-matched HUF 2s5s steepener to isolate policy easing from sovereign term risk.

Brazil: receive Jan-28 DI selectively; Jan-27 already embeds the obvious next cut. Avoid large outright long-end receivers until debt stabilization improves.

Shared failure mode: fiscal slippage weakens the currency, raises inflation expectations and prevents the central bank from validating the curve rally.
