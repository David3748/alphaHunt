KRUK vs. First Solar

Two apparent low-multiple longs, two very different cash engines. KRUK monetizes the gap between distressed-debt purchase prices and realized collections. First Solar monetizes scarce non-China solar capacity, U.S. manufacturing credits, and now a potentially transformative import-price floor. This report strips out the accounting optics, values the underlying engines, and defines the trade.

Prices: KRUK PLN 406.00 (WSE close, 28 Aug) · FSLR $204.46 (Nasdaq close, 28 Aug). All estimates marked as Alphahunt calculations, not company guidance.
Executive verdict

KRUK is the cleaner dislocation. FSLR is the bigger option.

KRUK offers better underwritten expected value at today’s price. FSLR has more explosive upside if the 38¢/W floor survives and clears into new U.S. contracts—but the current earnings multiple substantially capitalizes a temporary tax-credit stream.

Deep dive I

KRUK: a credit-underwriting factory disguised as a collection agency

KRUK buys pools of defaulted consumer receivables for a fraction of face value, then collects over many years. The economic moat is data, legal workflow, servicing cost, and the discipline to avoid overpaying—not simply the ability to call borrowers.

The cash engine

Purchase price + servicing cost + financing cost
→ discounted expected collection curve
→ realized cash collections
→ reinvest excess collections into new pools

Value creation = collections above all-in underwriting + scale/data advantage − dilution from overpaying

H1 2026: PLN 2.011bn recoveries, PLN 864m new purchases, PLN 1.398bn cash EBITDA, PLN 555m net income. Recoveries grew 5% YoY and beat the accounting curve by PLN 79m (down from PLN 127m a year earlier). Cash EBITDA grew 8% even though net profit fell 5% — a collections scale signal, not distributable earnings (see below).

The metric trap

Recoveries are not revenue. They include both interest income and return of the portfolio asset. Cash EBITDA is not distributable EBITDA. It adds collections back to accounting EBITDA, but excludes the recurring capital needed to replace runoff portfolios.

The most honest owner-earnings proxy is therefore:

cash collections − servicing cash cost − cash debt interest and cash tax
− maintenance portfolio purchases − maintenance capex

“Maintenance purchases” are unobservable and cyclical. That is why a single EV/cash-EBITDA multiple overstates value.

The country call

  • Poland: best economics, but market purchase price reached 34% of face in H1 as asset quality and bidder activity rose. KRUK’s primary unsecured share fell to 19%. Discipline matters more than volume.
  • Romania: cash EBITDA +5%, yet revenue −25% as revaluation fell and RON weakness caused much of the PLN 59m FX hit. This is the clearest normalization lever—and a warning about reported mark volatility.
  • Italy: the growth engine. Purchases +108%, recoveries +17%, cash EBITDA +24%; 55% share of primary unsecured spend. Returns are lower than Poland, but scale is building.
  • Spain: collections +5% and Q2 improved; investment is only selectively resuming after prior underwriting/operations problems. Treat as an option, not base-case profit.

Accounting stress test

KRUK fixes each portfolio’s credit-adjusted effective interest rate at purchase. Every quarter, it revises expected future cash collections. Better curves create income now; worse curves create charges. The H1 revaluation (PLN 226m) alone was 38% of pre-tax profit; adding the realized recovery deviation (PLN 79m — cash, not income) brings curve effects to PLN 305m, about half of pre-tax profit.

PLN 305m curve effectsPLN 285m other PBT

This does not mean 52% of profit is “fake” — but the margin of safety must include collection-curve error, and note the ~6% H1 effective tax rate is unusually low. Most Q1 unsecured revaluation related to cash flows through May 2033, making verification slow.

Balance-sheet endurance

  • PLN 5.5bn equity; PLN 7.3bn net debt; equity funds 41% of assets.
  • PLN 4.5bn available credit facilities, PLN 0.9bn undrawn at June 30.
  • Cash EBITDA/interest ~6.7× versus a 4.0× covenant minimum.
  • PLN 4.03bn notional interest hedges; H1 profit contribution PLN 53m.
  • Net currency exposure is structural: EUR/RON assets (Italy + Spain + Romania ≈ 61% of carrying value) against PLN debt; a 5% RON devaluation ≈ PLN 100m (~9% of normalized NI).
  • New seven-year bond priced at 3M WIBOR +250bp, the company’s lowest comparable margin.
  • Estimated collections are front-loaded enough to cover near maturities, but refinancing is structural: portfolios run for decades while debt is shorter.

Why it works

  • Collections keep beating the curve, but the beat narrowed (PLN 127m → PLN 79m H1/H1) — watch the trend, not the level.
  • Italy scale offsets Romanian mark normalization.
  • Lower Polish/Romanian rates and tighter bond spreads reduce funding drag.
  • PLN 500m digital program lowers collection cost after the investment phase.
  • 30%+ payout creates a valuation floor while leverage remains under 3×.

Why it fails

  • Portfolio IRRs compress as banks sell better-quality NPLs at richer prices.
  • Past positive curve revisions reverse; Level-3 assumptions prove optimistic.
  • Spain consumes capital again without producing target returns.
  • RON/EUR or PLN volatility hits translated earnings; higher-for-longer WIBOR/EURIBOR compresses NIM on the fixed-EIR book (the PLN 4.03bn interest hedges cover only a fraction).
  • Debt-collection regulation or court bottlenecks delay cash.

Variant perception

The market is focusing on net profit −5%, Romanian profitability collapsing from 41% to 30%, and no obvious near-term catalyst. The variant view is narrower: realized cash and collection efficiency are improving, the FX/revaluation components are mean-reverting, and the market is paying only ~PLN 1.4 for each PLN 1 of equity supporting a 19% ROE.

2026 guidance is a policy cash-flow statement

$2.145bn 45X$0.355bn other GP

At guidance midpoints, Section 45X credits equal 86% of gross profit: $2.145bn of $2.5bn. That is roughly $20 per share (a tax credit is not taxed income). Credits phase down 75%/50%/25% in 2030–32 and end after 2032.

This decomposition is simple but revealing: reported gross profit less guided 45X. It does not add back $115–135m underutilization or $90–100m start-up expense, which means clean ex-45X gross profit is even more credit-dependent than 86% until factories ramp.

The 38¢/W wedge

Backlog ASP
30.2¢
Import floor
38.0¢

The 7.8¢/W gap is a 26% umbrella above the blended backlog ASP; measured against new-booking ASP (~32–34¢) the realistic capture is ~4–6¢/W. A 15% ad valorem duty stacks on top of the floor, alongside existing AD/CVD. If FSLR captured only 3¢/W on 15GW of future annual U.S. shipments, gross profit would rise ~$450m before demand response. Full 7.8¢ capture would be ~$1.17bn—but that is not a credible base case.

Backlog quality: strong, not a bond

  • 45.1GW and $13.6bn base transaction value through 2030, down from 47.9GW at Q1 as shipments and terminations ran through.
  • 21.6GW carries technology-improvement adjustments worth up to $0.5bn—about 2.3¢/W on affected volume, largely 2027–28.
  • Contracts may include liquidated damages for late delivery or domestic-content failure.
  • Trade-policy clauses can cancel certain imported-module contracts without liability and return deposits.
  • Lightsource/BP dispute: FSLR claims $384.6m termination payments; litigation demonstrates that “contracted” does not equal collected.

Earnings-to-cash warning

H1 net income was $769m, yet operating cash flow was negative $360m. The gap came primarily from a $649m increase in government-grant receivables, $306m inventory build, supplier payments and deferred-revenue runoff. Capex added another $280m outflow.

Why it works

  • Dec 4 implementation is enforced broadly with few exemptions.
  • New U.S. contracts reset several cents above backlog ASP.
  • U.S. factories exceed 17GW annual nameplate after ramp.
  • 45X eligibility is not FEOC-threatened (thin-film has no polysilicon); the FEOC domestic-content ramp (solar MACR 50%→85% non-PFE by 2030) raises c-Si rivals’ costs and steers developers toward FSLR’s domestic content.
  • AI/data-center load supports procurement volume only if long-end rates stay low enough to keep PPA economics viable.

Why it fails

  • Grandfathering and onshoring exemptions hollow out the floor.
  • 38¢ makes marginal solar uneconomic; bookings slow before prices rise.
  • Post-2032 core margins never replace phased-out credits.
  • Series 7 remediation exceeds the current $40–65m loss range.
  • International facilities remain underutilized or impaired.
  • Higher long rates raise solar’s cost of capital and shrink the PPA buyer pool at the same time the floor raises module price.

Variant perception

The market alternates between valuing FSLR as a policy annuity and as a solar manufacturer. The better model treats 45X as a declining bond, the existing backlog as contracted but cancellable manufacturing work, and the MIP as a call option whose payoff is price capture multiplied by surviving demand.

Portfolio decision

Same framework, different risk budget

KRUK wins on valuation support and observable operating evidence. FSLR wins on convexity and catalyst intensity. Their idiosyncratic drivers diversify (Level-3 collection marks vs U.S. policy implementation); their macro drivers do not — both are rate-sensitive, risk-on exposures sharing a global real-rate and risk-appetite factor.

Do not force the old FSLR/RUN pair

RUN is exposed to residential financing, tax-equity availability and consumer rates; FSLR is utility-scale manufacturing. It is a useful “solar pain” hedge but introduces short-interest squeeze and business-model basis risk. A cleaner beta hedge is a calibrated short in TAN or a basket of import-sensitive module/project names. If the thesis is specifically that the MIP helps FSLR and hurts installers, use options to cap the short-side squeeze.

Best combined construction

A barbell of 1.5% KRUK + 1.0% FSLR expresses both a cash-underwritten value rerating and a policy-convex industrial event. Their main common factors are global real rates and risk appetite; only the idiosyncratic layer is genuinely uncorrelated. Size in USD risk terms — a PLN/USD forward hedges only the translation of a fixed PLN equity value, not KRUK’s economic RON/EUR exposure, so flag the residual currency mismatch unless the mandate is currency-hedged. Do not confuse the KRUK equity thesis with a PLN macro view.
