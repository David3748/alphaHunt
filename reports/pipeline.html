alphaHunt — the current pipeline

Generated 2026-08-22 · reflects src/century_pipeline.py, config/century.json, and the completed sealed 2019–2020 run

alphaHunt turns the SEC structured-filing feed into a point-in-time research dataset and tests one specific hypothesis: that an LLM reading only what was public at a filing's acceptance time can tell apart severely drawn-down companies that are safe from those that are not. The pipeline is eight resumable stages behind one command, with the trading rule frozen in writing before any model runs and post-signal prices held shut until every forecast exists.

One command

Every stage checkpoints to pipeline_state.json and is safe to re-run. Stopping and restarting is the normal way to operate it; completed stages are skipped. 

./play-century              # prompts for the API key without echoing; never writes it to disk
./play-century status       # counts + completed stages, no key required
python3 src/century_pipeline.py play --from-stage extract --through-stage evaluate

What the screen throws away

Eligibility is the pipeline's most consequential step, and it uses only pre-signal information. A case survives when it has ≥120 prior closes, a pre-signal close ≥ $1, 30-session average dollar volume ≥ $1M, a drawdown from the trailing 370-day high of −40% or worse, and no eligible filing from the same issuer in the prior 180 days. Future price availability is deliberately not a condition — that was the survivorship flaw in the earlier experiments this run was built to fix.

The model layer — seven calls per case

Each case costs five extraction calls plus two synthesis calls. Nothing in the prompt chain contains a price, a date after the cutoff, or an outcome. The evidence is the complete anchor primary filing as of its SEC acceptance time.

Five extraction lenses

Each lens receives a deterministic role-specific excerpt of the pack (term-weighted, so a 700k-character 10-K still gives the liquidity reader the liquidity pages). All five run against the same case and are redundant by design.

LensAsked for
liquiditycash runway, free cash flow, debt and maturities, covenants, dilution capacity, financing dependence
operationsrevenue and margin direction, backlog quality, demand, customers, unit economics, operating leverage
catalystsspecific funded events inside 90–180 days; dated catalysts separated from aspirations
accountingearnings quality, working capital, impairments, internal controls, auditor issues, non-GAAP adjustments
governanceincentives, ownership, related parties, litigation, listing risk, management credibility

Every claim must carry a short exact quote copied from the supplied text. Quotes are re-verified against the source after the call by whitespace-normalized substring match; ungrounded claims are dropped rather than repaired. Historically that has accepted roughly 64–69% of proposed claims.

Two independent syntheses

Two long-only researchers read the redundant extractions and each return a structured forecast: probability of positive 90-day excess return, probability of +20%, expected excess return, a downside-tail probability, a decision, thesis, catalyst, invalidation condition, and evidence references back to specific claims. Replicates are explicitly told not to imitate a presumed consensus.

The score

score = -mean(downside_tail_probability_pct across the 2 replicates) / 100

That is the whole ranking signal: safety first. Not upside, not expected return — the model's own estimate of how likely the tail is. A case with fewer than two valid syntheses is dropped, not imputed.

The frozen rule

PROTOCOL.md is written into the run directory before the first model call and is never rewritten if it already exists. Selection and execution are both fully specified there.

ElementRule
SelectionFirst 50 scores are warmup and select nothing. After that, accept a case if its score is at or above the expanding 90th percentile of strictly prior scores at its timestamp. Only past scores are ever in the threshold.
EntryNext eligible close after SEC acceptance. Same-day only if acceptance was before 16:00; otherwise the following session.
Sizing10% of current NAV per position, maximum 10 concurrent positions. Excess signals are skipped for capacity, not queued.
Hold90 calendar days, marked next-session daily.
Costs & cash25 bp per side; idle cash accrues at the historical 13-week Treasury yield (^IRX).
BenchmarkSPY, exposure-matched — the benchmark holds the same average capital exposure the strategy actually ran.
DelistingTwo bounds reported side by side: conservative marks a terminal price gap to zero, optimistic carries the last close forward.

Supporting statistics from the same run, none of which were gates:

  • HAC excess t = 2.30, two-sided p = 0.021; SPY beta 0.50; annualized regression alpha +32.0%.
  • Daily block bootstrap (5,000 draws, 20-session blocks): annualized excess 95% CI [+5.0%, +44.7%]; P(excess ≤ 0) = 0.006.
  • Trade bootstrap (10,000 draws): mean trade return 95% CI [+12.9%, +32.2%]; P(mean ≤ 0) = 0.000.
  • Trade win rate 71.7%; mean trade +22.1%, median +8.6% — the mean is well above the median, so a few large winners matter.
  • Zero terminal history gaps, which is why the conservative and optimistic bounds are identical here.

Read the result with its limits. 60 trades over two years, with 2020 supplying +49.0% CAGR against 2019's +4.1%, is a single favorable regime for a distressed-equity long. The pipeline removes the survivorship flaw at the eligibility step, but a Yahoo-sourced price layer still under-covers fully delisted securities — that is what the 21,093 "no price history" rows partly represent, and it is why the two terminal bounds exist. This is a validated research signal on an out-of-sample population, not a deployable strategy.

Where the results live afterward

JSONL is the run record; the bitemporal store in DuckDB is the queryable dataset. src/sealed_safety_ingest.py and comprehensive_lab.py ingest move cases, grounded claims, analyses, predictions, outcomes, and the backtest into research/alphahunt.duckdb, with large payloads as SHA-256 content-addressed blobs under research/blobs/ and a Parquet mirror in research/parquet/.

Every knowledge-bearing row separates effective_at (when the fact applies), available_at (when a market participant could know it), and system_from (when this database recorded it). Every prediction points at an immutable content-addressed input manifest, and a manifest rejects any document whose available_at is later than the prediction's knowledge time. That is the mechanism that makes a walk-forward backtest checkable rather than merely intended — the integrity validator re-hashes every blob and audits for manifest lookahead directly.
